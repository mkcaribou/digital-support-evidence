Caribou evidence review

Digital support changes how small businesses operate more often than it raises their sales

Caribou's evidence map brings together 30 impact evaluations of digital and data-first support for micro and small businesses, updated in October 2026. Each circle in the charts below is one result: one outcome for one program variant, 125 results in all.

A result shows a positive impact when the estimate is above zero and statistically significant at the 10 percent level or better, and a negative impact when it is below zero and significant. All other results show no impact, meaning the study could not distinguish the effect from zero.

Training and mentoring account for most of the evidence, and most of their sales and profit results show no impact

Results for business practices, sales and profits, by type of support. Each circle is one result.

Source: Caribou evidence map of digital and data-first support for micro and small businesses, updated October 2026. Types of support as grouped in the evidence map.

Most studies test training and mentoring delivered through phones, apps or video calls. These programs improve business practices in under half of their results, and raise sales or profits in about a quarter. Data tools, platforms and digital finance together account for nine studies and 12 sales and profit results, too few to support firm conclusions.

Human mentoring raises sales or profits far more often than remote training on its own

Sales and profit results only, grouped by how support reached the business. Each circle is one result.

Source: Caribou evidence map, updated October 2026. Delivery groups assigned by Caribou.

None of the 20 sales and profit results for remote training without a mentor shows a positive impact. Support that includes a person, whether a volunteer mentor, a consultant or an outsourced specialist, raised sales or profits in 11 of 24 results. Three of the mentoring studies draw on one sample of 930 Ugandan businesses, and three arms of a Nigerian study (consulting, insourcing and outsourcing) are not digital; without those three arms, the share is 8 of 18.

The AI mentor results come from a single study in Kenya, where businesses that were already performing well gained and weaker businesses lost. Bundled programs and stand-alone tools or finance fall between the two groups, with positive impacts in five of 11 and five of 12 results.

Of 65 sales and profit estimates, 35 lie within 10 percent of zero. Results that show no impact are not always small: two mentoring arms in Uganda raised sales by more than 20 percent on paper, but the estimates were too imprecise to rule out zero. Larger samples and longer follow-up would settle more of these cases.

The results point to four lessons for program design

  • Pair digital delivery with a person

    None of the 20 sales and profit results for remote training without a mentor shows a positive impact, against 11 of 24 for programs that include a mentor, adviser or specialist. Digital channels lower the cost of reaching businesses; contact with a person appears to be what changes results.

  • Treat better practices as an early sign of impact

    Business practices improved in 17 of 31 results, while sales rose in 12 of 37 and profits in 11 of 36. Programs that track only practices, such as record keeping or marketing, are likely to overstate their effect on business performance.

  • Look at who benefits as well as the average

    In Kenya, an AI mentor raised sales and profits for businesses that were already doing well and lowered them for weaker ones. In Brazil, chatbot-based training did the most harm to entrepreneurs with low digital literacy. Results for such groups belong in every evaluation report.

  • Plan evaluations large enough to detect realistic gains

    About half of the sales and profit estimates lie within 10 percent of zero, and 16 of the 29 studies that report a sample size cover fewer than 1,000 businesses. Sales and profits vary widely between small businesses, so modest gains need larger samples or repeated measurement to show up.

The evidence leaves gaps on resilience, AI, geography and gender

  • Resilience and risk

    Only one study reports whether businesses survived. None measures how businesses cope with climate shocks, cyber attacks or online fraud.

  • Artificial intelligence

    One study tests a generative AI tool: the AI mentor in Kenya. Two more test chatbot-based training in Brazil and AI-enabled training on an e-commerce platform in China. A registered trial in Pakistan that compares training on ChatGPT with training on Google search has not yet reported.

  • Geography

    Half of the 30 studies come from Africa, and 11 from East Africa alone, with Uganda contributing five. Asia accounts for five studies, Latin America for seven, and the Middle East and North Africa for one, in Jordan.

  • Women and other groups

    Eight studies work only with women, and six do not report the share of women in their sample. Only one study reports results separately for women and men (Batista et al., Mozambique).
